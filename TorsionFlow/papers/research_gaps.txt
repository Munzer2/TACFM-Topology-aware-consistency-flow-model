1. THE SHORT VERSION
======================================================================

- Combination gap: nobody has done 1-2 step conformer generation on the
  curved twist-angle (torsion) path. Every ingredient exists separately:
    curved path but slow ........ Torsional Diffusion (20 steps), GO-Flow (50)
    few-step but straight x,y,z . AvgFlow, EC-Conf, EnFlow (1-2 steps)
    few-step on tori, not molecules  RCM, Generalised Flow Maps
    torsional flow matching, other tasks  FlowPacker (side chains), MOFFlow-2
- Our multi-step twist-angle flow model (20 steps) already matches
  Torsional Diffusion's accuracy on GEOM-QM9 (AMR-R 0.182 vs 0.178).
- At 1 step it is barely better than random twists (AMR-R 0.236 vs 0.248).
  That 1-step gap is the open problem, and on QM9 it comes almost entirely
  from REAL multiple answers, not symmetry.

======================================================================
2. THE MAIN RESEARCH QUESTION
======================================================================

  When a few-step model fails on twist angles, how much of the failure
  comes from FAKE multiple answers and how much from REAL ones? Does
  removing the fake part make 1-2 step generation work?

Why 1-step fails at all: when one in-between shape can lead to several
correct final shapes, the model learns their AVERAGE, and one big jump lands
between them (a shape that is none of them).

Two kinds of "several correct answers":

  FAKE  Symmetric groups. A CH3 group looks identical every 120 degrees, so
        0, 120 and 240 are the SAME molecule. Also CF3, tert-butyl (120),
        phenyl rings and nitro groups (180).
        Fix: free and exact. Measure that twist modulo 360/k degrees.
        Detail: symmetry from both ends of a bond combines (a 3-fold group
        on one side and a 2-fold on the other repeats every 60 degrees).

  REAL  Genuinely different shapes, e.g. butane's anti (180) vs gauche (+-60).
        Fix: the model must learn to pick one -> few-step methods
        (Reflow, consistency, distillation, better pairing of noise and data).

4. THE QUESTIONS, WITH STATUS
======================================================================

Q1  Fake vs real: what share of the ambiguity is fake?           ANSWERED
    Toy notebook: 42% fake at the start of generation, 81% of that from CH3.
    All of GEOM-QM9 (108,563 molecules, no training; step3_probe/):
      - 30% of the ambiguity is fake, but 98.6% of that comes from
        hydrogen-only groups (CH3, OH...). The benchmark ignores hydrogens.
      - Counting only twists that move heavy atoms: 0.8% fake.
      - Only 2.3% of QM9 molecules have a heavy-atom symmetric group
        (inside those molecules, 38% of the ambiguity is fake).
      - Boltzmann weighting gives the same answer.
    => On QM9 the 1-step problem is almost all REAL multiple answers.

Q2  Does the symmetry fix help?                                   ANSWERED (toy)
    Toy, 5 seeds, exactly repeatable runs:
      - Molecules with heavy-atom symmetric groups: better at 1-2 steps
        (about 0.025-0.035 A AMR-R, 8-12%, 4-5 of 5 seeds).
      - Fixing CH3 groups too made plain chains WORSE at 1-2 steps
        (+0.025 A at 1 step, every seed).
      - Best version: fix heavy-atom groups only (keeps the gain, halves the
        cost; the rest of the cost comes through the shared network).
    On QM9 it cannot move scores much (Q1). It could matter on GEOM-Drugs
    (phenyl rings, CF3 common). Drugs deferred for now.

6. OTHER GAPS FOUND ALONG THE WAY (molecules)
======================================================================

- Curved vs straight at few steps (Q7). Only a fair test on GEOM-Drugs.
- A geometry audit: take published conformer methods and isolate whether
  the torus geometry is what actually helps.
- Wrap-around matters: RCM showed consistency training that ignores
  wrap-around falls apart on high-dimensional tori (error 35.16 vs 0.62 on
  a 128-dim torus).
- "Flows straight but not so fast": Reflow on curved spaces helped only at
  low step counts, sometimes hurt at high step counts, was expensive, and
  depended heavily on setup.

======================================================================
7. EARLIER GAPS FROM THE GRAPH WORK (Codes/SBM)
======================================================================

- Few-step consistency for DISCRETE data: largely CLOSED by 2024-2026
  (Di4C, DUO, PairFlow, Categorical Flow Maps, CDLM). Categorical Flow Maps
  does 1-step on QM9 molecular graphs. CDLM is text-only and lists graphs as
  future work. CDLM also argues (Prop. D.5, "comonotonic pseudo-bridge")
  that sharing one noise draw along the path - the tacm_v3 trick - causes
  low diversity.
- Coupling (pairing each noise sample with a similar-looking real graph):
  the probe measured 23% less target ambiguity; the training experiment
  gave 16% better 1-step quality, gradient noise unchanged.
- Geodesic vs straight paths: failed on graphs, but graphs are not really
  on the sphere. Open question: does geodesic help when the manifold is
  real (sphere data: earthquakes, volcanoes, floods, fires; SO(3))?
  Same instrument (geometry_ablation.py) on both.
- Benchmark audit (short separate paper): the broken MMD kernel, the
  degenerate Community_small dataset (212 distinct graphs out of 500), the
  confounded geometry comparison, and a memorisation check.
- Pre-training feasibility diagnostics: cheap model-free measurements that
  predict whether a training run can succeed.

======================================================================
8. CORRECTIONS TO THINGS SAID EARLIER
======================================================================

- "Twist-only models have a hard floor near AMR-R 0.17 A on QM9": wrong as
  a hard limit; we reached 0.164 on the first 100 test molecules.
- "The paper had the same RDKit embedding failures": impossible; 86 failed
  molecules caps COV-R at ~91.4%, and the paper reports 92.8%.
- "Our notebook uses Torsional Diffusion's exact settings": not the network
  type; their QM9 model uses use_second_order_repr: true, ours false.
